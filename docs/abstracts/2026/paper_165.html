20 1 Introduction 21 Road crashes remain a critical global public health challenge, claiming 1.19 million lives each 22 year. The burden falls disproportionately on low- and middle-income countries, which account 23 for 92 per cent of all road tra c fatalities despite having a fraction of the world's registered 24 vehicles (WHO, 2023). A substantial share of road crashes is attributable to inadequate road 25 infrastructure: poor geometric design, insu cient delineation, lack of pedestrian facilities, and 26 absence of appropriate roadside treatments are well-established risk factors for crash occurrence 27 and severity (Papadimitriou et al., 2019) . Designing safer roads and prioritising infrastructure 28 investment therefore requires accurate, large-scale road infrastructure assessment. 29 The International Road Assessment Programme (iRAP) provides a Star Rating System to assess 30 the inherent safety of roads, assigning ratings from 1 star (highest risk) to 5 stars (lowest risk) for 31 dierent road users (iRAP Star Rating Manual, 2024). To perform road infrastructure assessment, 32 trained and accredited human coders inspect geo-referenced road survey videos and record values 33 for more than 50 physical road attributes for each 100-metre segment in accordance with the iRAP 34 Coding Manual (iRAP Coding Manual, 2025). This attribute coding underpins iRAP's Star Rating 35 models, which translate infrastructure characteristics into evidence-based risk scores. These scores 36 are used by governments and road agencies worldwide to develop Safer Roads Investment Plans and 37 prioritise cost-eective safety interventions. However, the manual coding process remains costly, 38 time-consuming, and susceptible to coder variability, thereby limiting the scalability of formal road 39 assessments, particularly in low- and middle-income countries. 40 Recent research has begun to address this bottleneck through automated computer vision ap41 proaches. Deep learning models trained on road survey imagery have demonstrated promising 42 performance in classifying road attributes (Ka£an et al., 2024; Shahid et al., 2025b). However, 43 in our preliminary studies (Shahid et al., 2025b) we found that these road safety attributes are 44 structurally diverse, ranging from easily recognisable geometric features to more complex transient, 45 low-frequency scene elements that challenge any individual deep learning model. As a result, no 46 single deep learning model reliably excels across this full diversity of classi cation tasks simulta47 neously. 48 Ensemble learning oers a promising way to overcome this limitation. By combining predictions 49 from multiple independently trained models, ensemble methods can exploit complementary error

50 patterns and reduce task-speci c failure modes (Ganaie et al., 2022). Despite the well-established 51 e cacy of ensemble approaches in multi-task classi cation settings, their systematic application to 52 iRAP road safety attribute classi cation has not been reported in the literature. 53 To address this gap, this paper makes the following contributions: 54 1. We present the rst study to systematically apply and compare ensemble learning strategies 55 for automated multi-task iRAP road safety attribute classi cation from road survey videos, 56 covering 23 attributes across a large-scale dataset of 199,233 10-meter road segments. 57 2. We implement and evaluate various ensemble voting strategies spanning training-free ap58 proaches (Majority Vote, Con dence Vote), validation-calibrated methods (Weighted Av59 erage Probability, Task-Speci c Best), and a meta-learning strategy (Stacking), evaluated 60 against individual backbone models to quantify the bene t of each aggregation approach. 61 3. All strategies are assessed using comprehensive performance metrics providing a holistic view 62 of classi cation quality.

63 2 Methodology 64 Dataset Details 65 We evaluate our approach on a multi-task dataset comprising 199,233 annotated 10-meter road 66 segments extracted from survey videos covering 2,300 km of public roads in Bosnia and Herzegovina. 67 The dataset was created by aligning coded iRAP attributes (50+ attributes per segment) from an 68 o cial coding campaign with corresponding video frames using GPS endpoint information, as 69 described in (Shahid et al., 2025b). We use a subset of T = 23 iRAP classes and split the segments 70 into 186,796 training, 6,593 validation, and 5,844 test samples while keeping all segments from the 71 same video in the same partition to avoid data leakage.

104 Training Details 105 Both models are ne-tuned independently under an identical hyperparameter con guration to 106 ensure a fair comparison and controlled ensemble diversity. tting while retaining the best-performing checkpoint. Optimisation uses AdamW with β1 = 0.9, 109 β2 = 0.999, and ε = 10−8 on a single NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4080 GPU. A decoupled weight decay 110 of λ = 5×10−3 is applied to non-bias, non-normalisation parameters; bias and batch-normalisation 111 parameters are excluded from weight decay. The learning rate is set to 2 × 10−5 for both the 112 backbone and task-head parameters. Training employs mixed-precision arithmetic (FP16) via 113 PyTorch's automatic mixed-precision (AMP) with gradient scaling to prevent under ow. Gradients 114 are accumulated over 2 steps.
